# Executive Memo: U.S. Airline Operations, 2023-2025

## Decision summary

Operations became less reliable in 2025 even though total scheduled volume was slightly lower than 2024. The clearest portfolio-level opportunity is to protect later-day operations and reduce delay propagation, while airport/route intervention lists should be reviewed only after volume and network-mix controls.

## Findings and recommendations

1. **Later-day operations have materially higher observed delay risk.** Evening flights had a 28.7% arrival-delay rate and afternoon flights 27.5%, compared with 13.4% in the morning across the full period. **Implication:** disruption accumulates through the day. **Limitation:** time of day is also associated with route mix, weather, and schedule design. **Action:** prioritize aircraft/crew recovery buffers, turn-time monitoring, and constrained-airport recovery playbooks for afternoon/evening banks.

2. **2025 performance deteriorated.** On-time rate declined from 78.2% in 2023 to 76.3% in 2025, while arrival-delay rate increased from 20.3% to 21.9%; cancellation rate rose from 1.28% to 1.47%. **Implication:** the change is operationally meaningful at a seven-million-flight annual scale. **Limitation:** this descriptive trend does not isolate any one cause. **Action:** monitor month-by-month performance and segment the investigation by airport, route, and departure period before targeting resources.

3. **Delay propagation is the largest reported delay-minute category.** Late-aircraft delay accounted for 39.2% of reported delay minutes in 2025, ahead of carrier delay (32.5%) and NAS delay (21.9%). **Implication:** recovery from earlier disruptions is a high-value control point. **Limitation:** reported cause minutes are operational classifications, not experimental causal attribution. **Action:** evaluate aircraft rotations and connection-risk routes to identify where recovery buffers or swap/crew options are most needed.

4. **High-volume airport comparisons show concentrated operational pressure.** Among airports meeting the 5,000-departure threshold, FLL (26.8%), MIA (26.6%), DFW (26.0%), MCO (25.5%), and CLT (24.5%) had elevated observed delay rates; DFW and CLT also represent large departure volumes. **Implication:** high-volume hubs may merit deeper local diagnostics. **Limitation:** this is not a ranking of airport quality; weather, traffic management, route mix, and seasonality vary. **Action:** pair each airport review with time-of-day, season, and carrier/route cuts before operational action.

5. **Cancellations are concentrated at some weather-sensitive and constrained operations.** Aspen (ASE) had a 6.61% cancellation rate across 22,164 scheduled flights; among large metro airports, LGA (2.70%), EWR (2.61%), and DCA (2.33%) were also elevated. **Implication:** cancellation readiness should not be evaluated only from delay metrics. **Limitation:** airport conditions and carrier schedules differ substantially. **Action:** maintain separate cancellation/diversion monitoring with capacity, weather, and schedule-context inputs.

## Method note

The analysis covers 20.93M BTS-reported scheduled domestic flights from complete 2023-2025 monthly files. Arrival-delay/on-time metrics use operated, non-diverted flights; cancellation/diversion rates use all scheduled flight records. All statements are associational.
